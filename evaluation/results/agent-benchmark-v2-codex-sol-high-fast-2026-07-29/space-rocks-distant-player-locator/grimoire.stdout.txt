The smallest safe change is to keep distant-player position summaries on the existing `ship_delta` path, but send only absolute `id`/`x`/`y` samples at a low cadence. This preserves the current client-to-indicator pipeline, avoids a new packet schema, and does not restore 60 Hz updates for distant players.

## Verified current behavior

- Current receiver filtering is not implemented. World projection includes every player, and `buildIndependentHotLaneCandidates` projects and diffs all current ships without consulting receiver identity or position: `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/projection_world.go:16-38`, `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/lane_candidate_world.go:88-110`.

- The ship hot projection contains only movement-facing fields (`id`, `x`, `y`, `rotation`, `thrusting`), and its delta helper returns only update records: `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/hot_lane_projection.go:15-26`, `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/hot_lane_projection.go:54-60`. Newly entering filtered membership needs explicit handling because the generic delta classifies a previously absent record as a create, not an update: `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/delta.go:76-134`.

- The existing `ship_update` wire record already supports sparse `x`/`y` records and is used by `ship_delta`; no contract extension or generation is required: `shared/packets/realtime_wire.toml:772-796`, `shared/packets/realtime_wire.toml:1599-1601`.

- The client applies an assembled `ship_delta` through the normal ship-update merger: `client/scripts/protocol/realtime/world_lane_applier.gd:58-65`. That merger updates existing ships without dropping omitted rotation, presentation, health, or lifecycle fields: `client/scripts/protocol/realtime/world_lane_state.gd:144-177`. Existing tests specifically verify sparse field preservation: `client/tests/unit/protocol/realtime/test_world_lane_applier.gd:798-849`.

- World sync turns the merged ship state into render targets, interpolation exposes remote visual positions, and gameplay presentation passes those positions to `OSIndicatorController`: `client/scripts/world/world_sync.gd:91-99`, `client/scripts/world/world_sync.gd:196-213`, `client/legacy/player_render/player_sync.gd:119-176`, `client/legacy/player_render/player_sync_interpolation.gd:15-32`, `client/scripts/gameplay/gameplay_composition.gd:260-268`, `client/scripts/gameplay/presentation/gameplay_presentation_flow.gd:32-44`.

- The indicator controller hides onscreen targets, displays/clamps offscreen targets, and deletes an indicator only when its target disappears from the supplied position dictionary: `client/scripts/gameplay/presentation/os_indicator_controller.gd:25-59`.

Consequently, if only full-rate movement becomes receiver-filtered while full/lifecycle membership remains global, a distant player’s indicator remains present but its direction eventually becomes stale. If the broader interest change also removes distant players from full/lifecycle membership, this proposal is insufficient: hot updates cannot create their render nodes, and the indicator controller will remove them.

## Proposed design

In the receiver-interest implementation, divide active remote ships into:

- `fullRateShipIDs`: the receiver’s normal hot-interest set.
- `distantIndicatorShipIDs`: active remote players outside that set, excluding the receiver.

Then change ship candidate construction as follows:

1. Build normal full-rate transform deltas only for `fullRateShipIDs`.

2. Every 12 `HotLaneTick`s, append an absolute sparse record containing only `id`, `x`, and `y` for every `distantIndicatorShipID`. Twelve ticks gives a proposed 5 Hz fallback at the current 60 Hz server rate: `services/game-server/internal/constants/constants.go:5`, `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/baseline.go:27-35`, `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/active.go:40-43`.

3. Emit those summaries unconditionally on their cadence tick, even when the position has not changed since the previous summary. This makes a lost unreliable packet self-healing at the next sample instead of allowing delta suppression to preserve stale indicator state.

4. Keep summary records outside the full-rate ship delta baseline. They must not advance or alter filtered-interest membership. Newly entering full-rate interest must separately receive an immediate complete transform update.

5. Deduplicate by ID and preserve deterministic ID ordering before constructing the packet.

6. Feed the combined records into the existing `ShipWireDeltaPacket` and chunker. Existing chunking already applies the hard cap to ship update lists: `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/hot_lane_chunker.go:32-56`, `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/hot_lane_chunker.go:106-123`.

The 12-tick cadence is deliberately divisible by every current hot-lane cadence divisor—1, 2, 3, and 4—so a fallback tick is eligible even when chunk pressure has reduced the lane to 30, 20, or 15 Hz: `services/game-server/internal/protocol/realtime/scheduler.go:97-112`.

No client production files, packet TOML, or generated artifacts should change.

## Required acceptance coverage

- A moving nearby ship is eligible at normal hot cadence; a moving distant ship is absent on ticks 1–11 and included as `id`/`x`/`y` on tick 12.
- The receiver is never included in the fallback set.
- A ship cannot appear twice when interest changes on a summary tick.
- The same distant position is retransmitted at tick 24 even if unchanged.
- Dropping a summary does not affect later sequence acceptance; the next cadence sample repairs the position.
- Far-to-near transition sends an immediate full transform; near-to-far transition stops full-rate delivery but continues 5 Hz summaries.
- Deletion prevents a late summary from recreating a ship; current client behavior already covers this at `client/tests/unit/protocol/realtime/test_ship_hot_lanes.gd:79-101`.
- Multiple receiver session states produce different full-rate sets without sharing cadence or projection state.
- A large fallback cohort still passes the existing hot-lane hard-cap chunker.

## Design boundaries and rationale

“Reliable indicator” here means periodically self-healing presentation, not guaranteed transport delivery. `sr.ships` is explicitly unordered with zero retransmits: `services/game-server/internal/networking/webrtc_transport.go:30-40`. Moving fallback positions to `ships.lifecycle` or overlay would provide reliable delivery but would enlarge the schema/client surface and introduce stale cross-channel transform overwrites. Documentation also states that ordering is scoped to individual channels and does not order lifecycle against hot delivery: `docs/protocol/realtime-webrtc-gameplay-transport.md:98-108`.

Documentation rationale places future receiver-interest policy in realtime projection over immutable presentation input, not in the mutable simulation collision index: `docs/planning/protocol/realtime-protocol-architecture.md:146-152`. The proposed helper should therefore consume the interest result owned by that work rather than querying `game/spatial` itself.
